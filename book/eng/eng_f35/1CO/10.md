# 1 Corinthians 10

## Learn from Israel

**1** Now I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud and all passed through the sea

**2** —all were baptized into Moses by the cloud and by the sea—

**3** and all ate the same spiritual food

**4** and drank the same spiritual drink; because they kept drinking from a spiritual rock that accompanied them, and that Rock was the Christ.[^118]

**5** For all that, God was not pleased with most of them—their carcasses were scattered around the desert![^119]

## Examples

**6** Now these things became examples for us, to the intent that we should not lust after evil things as they also lusted.

**7** And do not become idolaters, just like some of them; as it is written: “The people sat down to eat and drink, and got up to amuse themselves.”[^120]

**8** And let us not fornicate, just as some of them did—and in one day twenty-three thousand died![^121]

**9** Neither let us test the Christ, just as also some of them did—and were destroyed by the snakes![^122]

**10** And do not grumble, just as also some of them did—and were executed by the destroyer![^123]

**11** Now all these things happened to them as examples and were written for our admonition, upon whom the ends[^124]of the ages have come.

**12** So then, let him who thinks he stands be careful that he does not fall!

**13** No testing[^125]has overtaken you except what is common to man, and God is competent,[^126]who will not allow you to be tested beyond what you can stand, but with the testing will also provide the way out, that you may be able to endure it.[^127]

## Eschew idolatry!

**14** Therefore, my beloved, flee from idolatry!

**15** I speak as to wise men; judge for yourselves what I say.[^128]

**16** The cup of blessing that we bless,[^129]is it not a sharing of the blood of Christ?[^130]The bread that we break, is it not a sharing of the body of Christ?[^131]

**17** Since the loaf is one, we, the many, are one body, because we all partake of that single loaf.[^132]

**18** Consider the physical Israel: are not those who eat the sacrifices partakers of the altar?

## Idol = demon

**19** So what am I getting at? That an idol is anything, or what is offered to idols is anything?

**20** Rather, that the things pagans sacrifice they sacrifice to demons and not to God. I do not want you to become participants with the demons.[^133]

**21** You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons; you cannot partake of the Lord's table and of the table of demons.

**22** Are we going to provoke the Lord's jealousy? Are we stronger than He?

## About conscience

**23** All things are ‘permissible’ for me,[^134]but not all things are expedient. All things are ‘permissible’ for me, but not all things edify.

**24** Let no one seek his own, but each one the other's well-being.

**25** Eat whatever is being sold in the meat market, asking no question for the sake of conscience;

**26** since “the earth is the Lord's, and all its fullness.”[^135]

**27** If some unbeliever invites you and you decide to go, eat whatever is set before you, asking no questions for the sake of conscience.

**28** But if anyone says, “This was offered to idols,” do not eat it for the sake of the speaker and of the conscience; since “the earth is the Lord's, and all its fullness.”[^136]

**29** ‘Conscience’ I say, not your own but that of the other. Do all to the glory of God Now just why should my freedom be judged by another's conscience?

**30** If I partake with thanks, why be slandered over something I give thanks for?[^137]

**31** Therefore, whether you eat or you drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God.[^138]

**32** Give no offense, either to Jews or to Greeks or to the Church of God[^139]

**33** —even as I try to please everyone in every way, not seeking my own profit but that of the many, that they may be saved.

[^118]: I would say that wherever Jehovah appears in the Old Testament it is Jehovah the Son, who became the Christ at His incarnation. But in what sense did they keep “drinking from a spiritual rock that accompanied them”? The physical rock did not move around, presumably, but the “spiritual rock” did.
[^119]: The apostle is not concerned to spare our sensibilities; quite the contrary. He is issuing a very strong warning. God's blessing does not necessarily indicate His approval.

[^121]: See Numbers 25:1-9. Another thousand died after the first day, giving a total of twenty-four thousand deaths.
[^122]: See Numbers 21:6-9.

[^124]: One would expect the singular here, but the Text is plainly plural. Well, I suppose each of the ages (plural) would have a distinct end, and the effect could be cumulative.
[^125]: The Greek word here can mean either ‘test’ or ‘tempt’ (test with a moral nuance), depending on the context. James 1:13 states plainly that God does not tempt us, and since in this context God is clearly involved, the correct choice is ‘test’.
[^126]: The best line of transmission (albeit representing only a third of the manuscripts) has ‘competent’ rather than ‘faithful’; that fits very well with the idea of testing, especially if it is God who is doing it.
[^127]: This is an important declaration, although I suppose we have all had moments when we wondered if we would continue to ‘stand’. The Text affirms that there will always be a way out—not always easy to ‘see’.
[^128]: This is cataphoric; they are to judge what follows.
[^129]: Those officiating bless the ‘cup’, which then blesses those who partake. Yes, to partake worthily brings blessing.
[^130]: Although one finds it difficult to agree with the Roman doctrine of transubstantiation, that the words of the one officiating transform the wine into literal blood, Paul evidently takes a very serious view of the ‘Lord's Table’. It is not a mere symbolism.
[^131]: “The body of Christ” here refers to His physical body on the cross, just as “the blood of Christ” refers to His blood shed on the cross.
[^132]: Evidently they did not cut/break the bread up into little pieces beforehand—there was a whole loaf on the table.
[^133]: Obviously demons exist. The visible idols represent invisible demons. When you worship an idol you subordinate yourself to its demon. There are ‘idols’ that are not physical images.
[^134]: Perhaps 3% of the Greek manuscripts omit “for me” (both times in this verse), to be followed by NIV, NASB, LB, TEV, etc. The difference in meaning is significant.

If we really want to please God, we will give an honest answer and act accordingly.
